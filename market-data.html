Short-stay lending and rental performance, by the numbers

Short-term rental bookings, occupancy and rates, plus the loan terms that finance them, from published market reports and lender program guides.

Two different kinds of numbers sit behind this name. One measures what short-stay properties earn: bookings, occupancy, nightly rates, and revenue per available night. The other measures what lenders will lend against those earnings: coverage ratios, down payments, loan-to-value caps, and program terms. Both matter, and neither substitutes for the other — a strong bookings market does not make a thin-ratio loan safe, and a generous loan program does not make a soft market strong. This page keeps the two families in separate, labeled rows, and keeps differently-defined market totals apart: Phocuswright counts gross bookings (the transaction value of stays booked), while Grand View counts market revenue under its own definition. The two series are never blended here.

What the 2025 numbers show is a market that has stopped expanding by adding listings and started growing by filling the ones it has — and a lending market that has standardized around that reality: supply growth fell to its weakest rate on record in mid-2025, full-year occupancy still ticked up, and DSCR programs converged on published ratio, down-payment, and reserve thresholds.

How to read these numbers

Included: Phocuswright global, North America, and Europe gross-booking figures (July 2026 report summary); Grand View Research U.S. revenue and segment figures (March 2026); AirDNA measured U.S. occupancy, rate, supply, and December 2025 snapshot data (January 2026); and lender-published program terms from two specialist lenders plus 2026 DSCR guide ranges. Excluded: any market total that blends revenue and gross-booking definitions, undated homepage claims presented as dated figures, and any rate figure presented as a current quote.

Forecasts are labeled as forecasts wherever they appear. Lender program terms are lender-published, not independently audited, and are shown with their sources so a reader can judge their age. Rate snapshots are dated and explicitly not current quotes.
